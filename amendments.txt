First, the honest diagnosis
Your copy right now is agency-generic. Phrases like "We engineer intelligent systems," "From idea to a system that runs your business," and "Four ways we move you forward" could belong to any of 10,000 AI agencies. Nothing tells a visitor what they walk away with or why you specifically. The neuroscience of conversion is blunt about this: the brain's threat-detection system (the amygdala) scans for risk and relevance in the first 3–5 seconds. Vague abstraction reads as risk. Specificity reads as safety.
Three principles drive everything below:
The brain is loss-averse (Kahneman's prospect theory — losses feel ~2x more painful than equivalent gains feel good), so naming what they're losing right now outperforms naming what they'll gain. It craves cognitive ease (Daniel Kahneman again — fluent, concrete language gets processed as more true and more trustworthy), so short, plain words beat jargon. And it responds to specificity and proof — concrete numbers light up differently than abstractions and lower perceived risk.
